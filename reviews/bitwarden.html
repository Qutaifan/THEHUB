Bitwarden Review (2026) — Honest Take for Free Users

Freeopen-sourceOpen Source

Bitwarden, from Bitwarden, is a free and open-source alternative to paid desktop software. On its Open Source plan it is genuinely usable, not a teaser.

Below: what you actually get for free, where the limits sit, and who should skip it.

What it does well

  • Real opensource capability on the Open Source tier — no credit card to start.
  • Practical for everyday use; outputs are usable, not just demos.
  • Actively maintained by Bitwarden; Password mgr is a current highlight.

Pros

  • Unlimited passwords on unlimited devices, free — the two limits most rivals use to push you to a paid plan simply are not here.
  • Open source and independently audited, with the server code published so the encryption claims can be checked rather than trusted.
  • Passkey storage, encrypted export and Bitwarden Send are all on the free tier, so you are not locked in and can leave with your data.
  • Self-hosting is supported if you would rather your vault never touched someone else's server.

Cons

  • Storing your TOTP two-factor codes requires Premium. A password manager that will not hold your 2FA codes without payment is the free tier's real ceiling.
  • Free sharing is capped at one other person. Households of three or more are pushed to the Families plan.
  • Premium is $19.80/year as of 2026, up from roughly $10. Most roundups still quote the old figure — check the vendor page before budgeting.
  • Emergency access, vault health reports and hardware-key support are all paid, so the free tier is weakest exactly where account recovery matters.

What it costs

Free covers unlimited passwords, unlimited devices, passkeys, encrypted export, Bitwarden Send and sharing with one other person. For a single user who keeps 2FA in a separate app, that is a complete product with nothing withheld.

Premium is $1.65/month billed annually at $19.80, and adds integrated TOTP, 5 GB of encrypted attachments, emergency access, vault health reports and up to 10 hardware security keys. Families is $3.99/month for six accounts, billed annually at $47.88.

Worth stating plainly: Bitwarden is open source and sells paid tiers. Being open source does not mean there is nothing to buy. Figures from Bitwarden's own pricing page, checked 2026-08-15.

Best alternatives

  • KeePassXC — Fully local, encrypted vault file — no account, no cloud, no subscription (security).
  • Proton Pass — Privacy-focused free tier with unlimited logins and hide-my-email aliases (security).
  • Google Password Manager — Built into Chrome and Android — free, zero setup, passkey support (security).

FAQ

Is Bitwarden free? Yes — it is open source and free to use in full.

Do I need a credit card? No, to start.

Best for? a free and open-source alternative to paid desktop software without paying.
