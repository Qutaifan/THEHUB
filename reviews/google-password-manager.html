Google Password Manager Review (2026) — Honest Take for Free Users

FreeGoogle

Google Password Manager, from Google, is a free password manager that secures your logins. It is free to use, with no paywall on the core experience.

Below: what you actually get for free, where the limits sit, and who should skip it.

What it does well

  • Free with an account you almost certainly already have, and it follows Chrome onto Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS.
  • A dedicated Password Manager app since late 2025, instead of living only inside browser settings.
  • Passkey support with cross-device sync, and Chrome flags credentials caught in known breaches.
  • Optional on-device encryption means Google cannot read the vault — off by default, worth turning on.

Pros

  • No-cost entry with real features.
  • Backed by Google, so it is actively maintained.
  • Good fit for users who want a free password manager that secures your logins without paying.

Cons

  • The vault is tied to your Google account and, in practice, to Chrome. Switching browsers means dragging your passwords with you.
  • The iOS app is more limited than the Android one because of platform restrictions, so a mixed household gets an uneven experience.
  • Turning on on-device encryption complicates recovery: forget the passphrase and Google genuinely cannot help.
  • No secure notes, no aliases, no real sharing — it stores passwords and stops there.

What it costs

No money, and no paid tier exists.

What you pay is consolidation risk. Your email, your browser and your password vault sit behind a single account, so losing access to that account — or having it compromised — takes all three at once. For some people that trade is fine; it should at least be a decision rather than a default.

Best alternatives

  • Bitwarden — Unlimited passwords and devices on the free plan, zero-knowledge encryption (security).
  • KeePassXC — Fully local, encrypted vault file — no account, no cloud, no subscription (security).
  • Proton Pass — Privacy-focused free tier with unlimited logins and hide-my-email aliases (security).

FAQ

Do I need a card? No, to start.
