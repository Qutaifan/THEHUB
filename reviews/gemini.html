Gemini Review (2026) — What Google's Free Tier Costs You Instead of Money

FreeGoogle

Gemini is the only major assistant on this list that most people already have an account for. If you have a Gmail address you have Gemini, at no charge, with no card and no separate signup. That convenience is the whole product strategy, and it is also the reason the interesting question about Gemini is not "how good is it" but "what is Google getting in return".

The answer is in Google's own support documentation rather than in any review: on the default settings, the free tier is paid for with your conversation history.

What the free plan actually gives you

  • A general assistant for drafting, summarising, explaining and brainstorming, reachable from the web app, the phone app and increasingly from inside Search and Workspace.
  • Multimodal input — you can hand it an image, a file or a screen and ask about it, not just type at it.
  • A working allowance of Google's mid-tier models, with the flagship Pro model available in small quantities before it cuts over to a lighter one.
  • No credit card, no trial clock, no cancellation to remember.

Google does not publish a fixed number of free messages per day, and the figure has moved more than once. Treat any specific cap you read elsewhere as a snapshot rather than a policy.

The real price: your chats train the model

This is the part worth reading twice, because it is the difference between Gemini and a paid or self-hosted assistant, and it is stated plainly by Google rather than inferred by us.

With the Keep Activity setting switched on — which is the default — your chats and the files, photos and screens you share are saved to your Activity and used to train Google's models. Google also states that a subset of conversations is read by human reviewers to improve its services, and that those conversations are disconnected from your account before being sent to service providers.

You can turn Keep Activity off. Google is explicit that even then it still uses your chats to respond to you and to protect the service, so "off" means "not retained for training", not "not processed". The setting is opt-out and forward-looking, which means the default costs you something before you have made any decision at all.

For homework, recipes and first drafts this is a fair trade. For a client contract, an unreleased product spec or anything covered by an NDA, it is not, and no configuration screen changes that.

Where the paywall actually falls

Google sells three consumer tiers above free — AI Plus, AI Pro and AI Ultra — and they are bundled with Google One storage rather than sold as pure model access, which makes straight comparison with a flat chatbot subscription harder than it looks.

What the money buys is roughly: a larger allowance of the same assistant, guaranteed access to the current flagship Pro model rather than occasional access, Deep Research for long multi-source reports, and at the top tier the Deep Think reasoning mode. Storage scales with the tier, from a few hundred gigabytes at the entry plan into the terabytes higher up.

The honest read is that upgrading is a storage-and-allowance decision more than a capability decision. If you are not hitting the free ceiling and you are not doing long research runs, the free tier is the same assistant.

Pros

  • Zero friction if you already have a Google account — nothing to install, nothing to sign up for.
  • Genuinely multimodal on the free plan, including images and file uploads.
  • Tied into Search, Android and Workspace, so it turns up where you already are.
  • Google publishes its data-handling terms clearly enough that you can make an informed choice.

Cons

  • Training on your chats is the default state, not an opt-in.
  • A subset of conversations is reviewed by humans, which rules the free tier out for confidential material.
  • Free allowances are undocumented and have changed repeatedly, so you cannot plan around them.
  • Upgrade tiers are bundled with cloud storage, which muddies what you are actually paying for.
  • Deep integration with Google is a lock-in as much as a convenience.

Best alternatives

  • ChatGPT — the closest like-for-like free assistant, with a comparable training default you can also switch off (chatbots).
  • Perplexity AI — better choice when you want cited sources rather than a confident paragraph (chatbots).
  • DeepSeek Chat — free and capable, but moves the privacy question to a different jurisdiction rather than solving it (chatbots).

FAQ

Is Gemini free, and do I need a credit card? The Gemini app is free with an ordinary Google account and no card. Google sells paid tiers (AI Plus, AI Pro, AI Ultra) that raise your usage allowance and unlock the top Pro models, Deep Research and Deep Think, but nothing is charged unless you subscribe.

Does Google train its models on my Gemini chats? Yes, on the default settings. Google's own documentation states that with the Keep Activity setting on, your chats and anything you share are saved to your Activity and used to train its models, and that a subset of chats is read by human reviewers after being disconnected from your account. Turning Keep Activity off stops that, though Google says chats are still used to respond to you and to protect the service.

How do I stop my chats being used for training? Turn off Keep Activity in the Gemini app's Activity settings. It is opt-out rather than opt-in, so it stays on until you change it, and it applies going forward rather than retroactively. If a conversation is confidential, the safer answer is not to put it into a consumer chatbot at all.
