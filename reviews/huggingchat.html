HuggingChat Review (2026) — It Shut Down, Then Came Back as Omni

Free tieropen sourceHugging Face

Most reviews of free software do not have to open by establishing that the software exists. This one does. Hugging Face closed the original HuggingChat at the start of July 2025, offered users a zip export of their conversations, and said it was going to build something more integrated. For three and a half months there was nothing at the address.

HuggingChat Omni launched on 16 October 2025 in the same place. It is a different product wearing the old name, and it is more interesting than what it replaced.

How Omni is different from the thing that closed

The old HuggingChat was a chat interface with a model dropdown. You picked from a short list of open models, and the quality of your answer depended on whether you had picked well — which required knowing things about open models that most people reasonably do not.

Omni removes the dropdown from the critical path. A routing model reads your request and dispatches it to whichever model in the catalogue suits that kind of work, drawing on a catalogue that has grown past a hundred models served through Hugging Face Inference across many providers. Coding goes somewhere good at code, translation goes to a translation model, general questions go to a general one.

The detail that makes this respectable rather than opaque is that it tells you what it did: the name of the model handling your request appears while the answer streams. You are not being quietly downgraded to something cheap, and if you disagree with the router you can still pick a model yourself.

The credits, which is where free stops

Free here means metered, not unlimited. Usage runs on Hugging Face inference credits: signed-in free accounts get a modest monthly allowance, and PRO subscribers get a substantially larger one. Heavy use on a free account will run out.

That is a fair arrangement and an unusually transparent one — running inference over a hundred models across multiple providers costs real money, and a credit balance is a more honest way to express that than an undocumented daily cap. It does mean this is not the tool for all-day conversational use without paying, and anyone who arrives expecting an unlimited free ChatGPT will be disappointed by the end of the month.

The part that actually matters: you can take it with you

The chat interface is open source, and Hugging Face keeps it that way — the announcement was explicit that Omni is 100 percent open source and self-hostable from its GitHub repository.

Given that this product has already been shut down once, that is not a box-ticking exercise. Every other assistant covered here can be discontinued, repriced or repositioned, and your only recourse is to export what you can and leave. Here the software survives the service. You can run the same interface against your own inference, or a provider you choose, and nothing about Hugging Face's product strategy can take it away from you.

That is the honest case for HuggingChat in 2026: not that it is the strongest free assistant, because it is not, but that it is the only one on this list where the vendor cannot unilaterally end your access to the software.

Pros

  • Automatic routing to a suitable open model, with the model used shown to you rather than hidden.
  • Access to a very wide catalogue of open models through one interface and one account.
  • Interface is open source and self-hostable, so the software outlives the hosted service.
  • Credit-based metering is more transparent than an undisclosed message cap.
  • No card needed to start.

Cons

  • It was discontinued once already, which is a fair thing to weigh before depending on it.
  • Free inference credits are modest and heavy users will exhaust them.
  • Router choices are not always the choice you would have made, and quality varies by model.
  • Less polished than the commercial assistants on mobile and on long conversations.
  • Self-hosting is a real option only if you can supply or pay for inference.

Best alternatives

  • Ollama — run open models locally with no service to shut down and no credits to spend (chatbots).
  • LM Studio — friendlier desktop route to the same local-model idea (chatbots).
  • Jan.ai — open-source desktop assistant for people who want the interface offline as well as the model (chatbots).

FAQ

Did HuggingChat shut down? It did, and then it came back. Hugging Face closed the original HuggingChat at the start of July 2025, letting users export their conversations as a zip file, and said it was working on something more integrated. HuggingChat Omni launched on 16 October 2025 at the same address. If you bookmarked it, walked away during the gap and assumed it was gone, it is running again.

What is Omni and how does the model routing work? Omni is a routing layer in front of a catalogue of open models served through Hugging Face Inference across many providers. Instead of you picking a model first, a routing model reads your request and sends it to the one best suited to it, and the interface shows you which model answered while the reply streams. You can still bypass the router and select a model yourself.

Is it genuinely free, and is it really open source? Free use runs on inference credits, with a modest allowance for signed-in free accounts and a substantially larger one for PRO subscribers, so it is metered rather than unlimited. The chat interface itself is genuinely open source and can be self-hosted from its GitHub repository, which is the meaningful difference from every other hosted assistant here: if the service closes again, the software does not go with it.
